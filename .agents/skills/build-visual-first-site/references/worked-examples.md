# Worked routing examples

These examples teach decisions, not a reusable visual template.

## Panoramic mountain photograph

**Evidence:** one wide landscape, open sky, distant subject, generous negative space; goal is an emotional destination landing page.

**Good route:** Poetic Immersive. Place concise copy in real sky or mist space, use one image-derived ink color, protect the horizon, and add one slow environmental motif already implied by the scene.

**Bad route:** center an oversized headline across the mountain, add glass cards and several floating controls, then call it premium.

## Mixed travel photographs

**Evidence:** twelve landscape and portrait images across several locations with dates and notes; goal is a personal trip journal.

**Good route:** Travel Editorial with one restrained Cinematic Story transition. Curate chapters, preserve metadata, and choose a distinct mobile sequence.

**Bad route:** crop every image into a full-screen hero or discard dates because the landscape image is attractive.

## Interior and architecture set

**Evidence:** six controlled photographs with strong lines, material details, and muted light; goal is a boutique-stay introduction.

**Good route:** Quiet Luxury. Use a precise grid, small metadata, material-led neutrals, and measured light or reveal motion.

**Bad route:** add drifting petals, script typography, or a dreamy landscape overlay unrelated to the photographs.

## Food, home, or personal-life set

**Evidence:** warm close-ups, people, texture, and everyday moments; goal is approachable storytelling.

**Good route:** Clear Lifestyle. Keep reading easy, surfaces open, and motion tied to steam, light, fabric, or small interactions.

**Bad route:** imitate a luxury product launch with black fields, tiny low-contrast text, and severe spacing.

## Deliberate scene sequence

**Evidence:** five images have a meaningful order from departure through arrival; goal is to make the progression felt.

**Good route:** Cinematic Story. Use acts, controlled disclosure, and slow transitions; keep each scene legible before advancing.

**Bad route:** use a masonry gallery that destroys order or autoplay rapid transitions that turn the story into decoration.

## Weak, small, or inconsistent images

**Evidence:** mixed low-resolution files, conflicting color, little crop tolerance.

**Good route:** select one viable anchor, use framed editorial placement, quiet fields, or small details, and state what would benefit from replacement.

**Bad route:** upscale every image to full-screen, hide defects under heavy blur, or generate replacements without permission.

## No images yet

**Evidence:** clear subject and goal, but no assets.

**Good route:** infer one evidence-based profile, create or source one hero direction, inspect it, then extend the family only if it works.

**Bad route:** generate a large visual set before proving the hero or fill the page with CSS gradients and abstract blobs that pretend to be imagery.
